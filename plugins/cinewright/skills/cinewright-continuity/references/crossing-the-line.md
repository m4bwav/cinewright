# Crossing the line

## Rules

Each method lets the next card sit on the other side of the line. Write its name as the card's reason for crossing.

- polar-shift: the pair look off together at a third point; cut to that point straight down a new line; come back on the other side (Arijon ch. 8).
- onlooker: a still watcher's close-up, head turning across the frame, sits between two cards of a traveller whose direction then flips. The second card reads as the watcher's view. Works with a group if all heads turn together (ch. 9).
- diversion: a foreground person walks out one side and enters the next card from the other, or the camera pans with a walker crossing the other way and holds on the far subject (ch. 9).
- vertical: one of a facing pair kneels or sits; cut on the finished downward move to the reverse from the far side, the still one first (ch. 9).
- same-half: the mover stays in the same frame half in both reverses, next to a still anchor (a person, car, tree) that also keeps its half. The flip then reads as continuous (ch. 9).
- vehicle close-up: a passenger turning their head side to side in a close-up with a blocked background joins side shots from opposite sides of the vehicle. Good for AI: no moving landscape needed (ch. 9).
- A 180 degree turn split across external reverses: card A starts it, centre to one side; card B finishes it from the other side to centre. Sitting and reclining across reverses flip their arc the same way and stay in the same half (ch. 11).

- Two parties that will meet (armies, riders, pursuer and quarry) each keep one travel direction, opposed, on every card until the card that shows both (ch. 9).

## Verify

- `continuity diff` AXIS findings name one of these methods in the card notes, or the cross is shown in camera.
