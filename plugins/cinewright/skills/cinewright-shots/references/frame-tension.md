# Frame tension

## Rules

- Compression and release: pressing the subject toward one edge, with the open space on the other side, reads as pressure. A centred subject reads as settled; keep it for calm or solemn beats (after Mateu-Mestre, *Framed Ink 2*).
- Lines of tension: write the two or three points that matter (eyes, a hand, the target) as frame positions. The wider apart and the nearer the edges, the higher the tension.
- Horizon and mood: a low horizon gives sky, freedom and scale; a high one gives weight and threat; mid-frame only for a solemn or symmetrical beat. Set it with `camera.horizon` (camera-angles vocab).
- Open framing (people cropped, objects in the foreground) pulls the viewer in; closed framing (everyone whole and balanced) keeps them at a distance. A doorway or a frame within the frame makes the viewer an eavesdropper. Choose one per scene and write it in the style bible's notes (after Katz, *Shot by Shot*).
- Crowds and battles get one flow shape in the prompt, pointed at the hero. For example: "the line of soldiers climbs from lower left to the banner, spears angled toward the king". Heads and weapons aim the eye (cinewright-movement for the crowd itself).
- Give empty space a job: "lone rider small in the lower right, empty sky filling the upper left" carries more than naming a composition rule.
- Aperture: show the key action through a small gap (a half-open door, between hanging cloth). The partial view makes the viewer lean in (Bordwell, *Figures Traced in Light*, ch. 3, 5).
- Intermittent visibility: a person passing through bars of shadow or behind posts builds a wish to see them again. Use for suspense (ch. 3).
- Withheld reaction: hide the face at the peak (back turned, behind a screen) and carry the emotion on the sound. It also dodges a model's weakest output, a big crying face (ch. 3).
- Recessional staging runs away to a vanishing point and pulls the eye down the road; planimetric staging strings figures parallel to the frame like a frieze (ch. 4).
- Figures seen three-quarters from behind mute the drama and push the viewer to the setting. Use only on purpose (ch. 4).

## Pitfalls

- A key element just touching an edge looks like an accident (`edge-tangent`); crop boldly through it or leave clear space.
- Two-shots with both people pressed to the edges read as a fight even when the scene is tender.

## Verify

- The brief or the style bible names the scene's framing (open or closed), and every outdoor card that matters for mood sets `camera.horizon`.
