# Scene setups

## Rules

Book recipes, unverified on video models. Every card still follows camera-moves (one move per card, speed stated). Treat a recipe as a starting card list, not a rule.

Fights:

- Faked hit: telephoto, both fighters in frame. The fist passes behind the victim's head and the head snaps back on that frame (MS1 1.1).
- Hero's one punch: camera near the victim, the hero centred and coming on. The camera eases back as if pushed, then halts and jolts sideways with the blow (MS1 1.2).
- Brawl drag: camera alongside at head height, locked to one fighter at a fixed distance. Change angle only when they hit the wall or the floor (MS1 1.3).
- Knockdown in one card: the camera drops to floor level while the attacker's body hides the fall and he keeps moving (MS1 1.4).
- Kicks: card A low and close on the aggressor; card B low, long lens on the victim recoiling. Cut on the third hit (MS1 1.5).
- Implied violence: waist-high camera looking up at the attacker striking down out of frame; never show the result (MS1 1.7).
- End of fight: the winner from below, the loser from above in a different light and lens (his point of view). Close on the winner alone after the loser drops out (MS1 1.6, 1.8).

Chases:

- Locked lead: short lens close to the runner at a constant distance and frame position, rough ground, turns, empty space behind (MS1 2.1).
- Crossing pan: long lens panning with the runner left to right, pursuers charging at the lens from the side, obstacles whipping past in the foreground. Use one pursuer only in a quiet setting (MS1 2.2).
- Squeeze: a short lens blocks the alley, slides aside and pans as the runner passes looking back. A calm look back and a shrinking runner means he escaped (MS1 2.3).
- Gain without gain (two cards, cross-cut): a handheld camera catches up behind the hero toward a goal that does not grow (long lens); a smooth slow pull back before the charging pursuer (short lens). Add an obstacle such as a jammed door (MS1 2.4, 2.9).
- False POV grab: a 4-5 s chase from behind the victim; the attacker overtakes from frame edge and grabs (MS1 2.7).
- Impossible gain (three cards): a walking pursuer is briefly hidden; a reaction on the hero; then a much longer lens, pursuer far nearer (MS1 2.8).
- Feet chase: low, behind the pursuer's legs, with the quarry offset right ahead. Use it briefly (MS1 2.10).

Car scenes:

- Moving dialogue: shoot each person from the far side of the car; driver hard frame-left, passenger hard frame-right. Long lens, slow street motion, the driver gets glances and a mirror close-up (MS1 9.1-9.3).
- Back seat: the passenger feels isolated, talking through the mirror and side glances (MS1 9.2).
- Parked: camera outside the open door or window on a long lens, both leaning forward; keep the car steady (MS1 9.4).
- Stake-out: very long lens through the side window. Focus on the far face, the near one soft, heads the same size; only one person in focus per card (MS1 9.7).
- Car beside a walker: one camera height for both directions picks whose scene it is; keep some bodywork in frame (MS1 9.6).

Arguments and duels:

- Circling: the dominant one walks side-on; the other shuffles sideways to keep facing him. Cards: POV from the dominant one, then an over-the-shoulder from behind the other (MS1 11.1).
- Attack: a steady push at the retreating person, framed hard to one side (MS1 11.2).
- Defend: a gentle pull back from the speaker as she rocks forward and back (MS1 11.3).
- Lunge: tight long-lens frame held still, the actor lunges a full foot in (MS1 11.4).
- Panic: a push that whips with a swung weapon at its speed (MS1 11.5).
- Truce: static at the actors' level, no pan (MS1 11.6).
- Crossing argument: short lens, paths crossing in half-circles (MS1 11.8).

## Pitfalls

- Two-camera timing and props matched across a cut (MS1 2.4, 5.9) will not hold between separate generations; accept a loose match.
- Set-safety notes (padding, platforms) do not apply.
