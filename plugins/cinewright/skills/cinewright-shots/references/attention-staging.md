# Attention staging

A 5 to 10 s generation is one held shot. These moves do the work a cut would.

## Rules

- Levers that pull the eye: faces, eyes, mouths and hands first, then contrast, frontality, movement, centre and light (Bordwell ch. 2). The beat's subject wins at least one lever and does not lose on all the others.
- A far figure loses to a near one by size. Win it back by being more central, more frontal while the near one turns away, or moving while the near one holds still. Write the fix into the action line (ch. 2).
- Turn away to hand over: a foreground person turning from camera passes attention deeper into the frame; turning back takes it back. This is the in-shot reaction cut (ch. 2).
- Turn to camera for a reverse without a cut: one person turns from the other toward the lens (Katz ch. 4).
- Block and reveal: a near person masks a door or a second person, then steps aside at the moment that matters. A small move near the lens hides or shows a large area far away. Write it as one motivated move (Bordwell ch. 2, 5).
- Clear a zone before an arrival: a region empties, then the new element appears there. Name the empty region in the prompt (ch. 2).
- Decentre, then recentre: the key person off centre builds tension; an arrival in the empty centre resolves it. Attention ending in the centre cell resolves; leaving it unsettles (ch. 2).
- A look points the eye: a detail in an outer cell gets a named glance toward it, then back (ch. 2).
- Step toward camera to underline a line, with no cut (Katz ch. 4). Move the actors to the camera before moving the camera to the actors (Katz ch. 5).
- Follow one person until they pass a second, then stay on the second: the hand-off move. Both are in the card's cast (Katz ch. 3-4).
- Rush to the foreground at the turn: the far person walks up to the near one's plane. A deep diagonal usually resolves into a row across the front (Bordwell ch. 2).
- Swap through the centre: the front person crosses away as the newcomer takes the old spot. Both moves happen at once (ch. 2). Unverified on video models.
- Cut only for what staging cannot show: a tiny object, a peripheral one, one blocked by a person in front. Then cut straight down the lens axis (ch. 2).
- A held shot needs its own arc. Write the beat so the last second is the strongest frame; it also seeds an extend (ch. 4).

## Numbers

- One attention hand-over per card. Hold about 2 s on the new subject before the card ends (after ch. 2: a staged shift prompts but cannot force, so it needs time).
- Two or more hand-overs ("turns to", "steps aside", "looks at") in a card under 8 s: too many.

## Pitfalls

- attention-lost: the subject is back-turned, still, off centre and dark while someone else faces camera and moves.
- Models may make passers-by or background extras too prominent; say "small" and "far".

## Verify

- Look pass: in the last two seconds, is the eye on the subject the card's attention end names, and what pulls it there?
