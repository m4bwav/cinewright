# cinewright-shots references

Generated by `cine.py kb index`; do not hand-edit. Open one entry with `python scripts/cine.py kb show <slug> --section <name>` (sections: Rules, Numbers, Vocabulary, Pitfalls, Verify, Notes).

- `attention-staging`: Steer the eye inside one shot by turning, moving, blocking and clearing instead of cutting; one hand-over per card, held about 2 s; the subject must win a lever.
- `blocking`: Where each person stands and moves in a scene, written from camera side A in the scene bible before any card, with staging in depth and a reason for every move.
- `camera-angles`: Vertical angle tokens (eye-level to dutch) with what each reads as and where its horizon sits, camera height, dutch pairs, the azimuth number for the 30-degree check, and framing tokens.
- `camera-moves`: The camera-move tokens a shot card allows (one per card), what each does physically, and the prompt words each compiles to.
- `coverage-plan`: Planning a scene's cards by weight: turn card first, coverage density, relation (isolate, connect, bind), rests in long moves, cutaway hooks, a fallback staging.
- `coverage`: Which shots a scene needs (master, matched singles, an insert), the classical card order, the close-up budget, and why AI coverage is a cut plan, not a pile of takes.
- `dialogue-triangle`: Name each dialogue card's setup in the triangle (apex, external, internal, parallel), cut only same-side pairs, size pairs, heads set the line, one-person gaze.
- `frame-tension`: Where the subject sits against the frame edges and the horizon, and what that does to the audience: compression and release, lines of tension, horizon and mood, open and closed framing, crowd flow.
- `framing`: Thirds, looking room, headroom, depth layers and Block's contrast and affinity, written as the position words a video prompt can carry.
- `group-order`: Three or more people across cuts: legal left-to-right order changes, pivots, the arbiter of attention, re-establishing, zones for regrouping casts, odd and even groups.
- `group-staging`: Three or more people: the I, A and L patterns, one axis per exchange, the middle person who must keep one side, and props between opposing close-ups.
- `point-of-view`: Whose scene each card serves (pov), the four questions to answer before tokens, how POV shots are set up, and the card's job in the edit (role).
- `scene-setups`: Book recipes for fights, chases, car scenes and arguments as starting card lists (one move per card), with what does not transfer to video models.
- `screen-direction`: The fixed phrases for travel, eyeline and frame position that every card and compiled prompt uses, so direction never drifts between shots.
- `shot-list-order`: The shot list is the cards; slate letters, cut order versus writing order, how beats map to shots, grouping shots into generations, and which shot to render first.
- `shot-sizes`: The eight shot-size tokens cards use (EWS to ECU), what each frames, the order used for size steps, and the prompt words each compiles to.
- `space-types`: Block's four kinds of screen space (deep, flat, limited, ambiguous): the staging, lens and move each needs, the words compile writes, and the SPACE warnings.
- `staging-moves`: Why and how people move in a scene: territory (advance, yield), the five reasons for a move, regroup resets, opening and closing on movement, three-card journeys.
- `thirty-degree-rule`: When a cut between two shots of the same subject reads as a jump cut, the numbers the diff checks, and how to fix one.
