# Group order

## Rules

- Write each card's on-screen order, frame left to right, in the notes: `[A, B, C]`.
- Three people: a straight line or an L keeps every screen side in both external reverses. A triangle cannot; pick one swap below (Arijon ch. 6).
- Legal order changes between two cards of one exchange: nobody moves; the dominant centre person stays and the flankers swap; the silent arbiter jumps sides and the talking pair keep order; the dominant edge person stays and the other two swap; at right angles, one end person jumps to the other end (ch. 6-7). Anything else needs a pivot.
- A pivot is a person on the same screen side in both cards, or a close-up of the dominant person between the reverses (ch. 6).
- The arbiter of attention: in a shot of three or more, silent people look at whoever holds the interest and turn their heads together when it moves. One person looking elsewhere splits attention. A speaker change inside a group needs a visible head turn (ch. 3, 6).
- Three-shot to a two-shot of the leads: cut as a reverse, not a push in on the same axis (ch. 6).
- Anyone who drops out of the closer cards is re-established before the scene ends, or exits on screen (ch. 8).
- Two who faced a third turn to each other: the line rotates 90 degrees. Show the turn in a card; it becomes the apex of the new triangle (ch. 6).
- Hiding a person behind a pillar, or leaving them out of both close reverses, lets the others jump sides without confusion. Note the true order (ch. 6).
- Several groups in one frame: the nearest dominates. Groups behind stay closed circles with small moves. To stress the front group, the back groups turn and look at it together (ch. 3).
- Odd groups keep one person at centre; even groups balance around the centre line. With a full front row, the edge people turn inward toward the centre pair (Bordwell ch. 2).
- In a seated group, small head and shoulder turns show the next reaction without a cut. That replaces some re-establishing wides (Bordwell ch. 2, 5).
- A fixed wide with everyone on one side of a table avoids most continuity seams in AI video. The cost: no cut-ins to tighten pace (Bordwell ch. 1).
- Four or more people who regroup: mark two or three zones in the location, give each zone its own wide submaster, then its coverage. Show zones from front and side angles only, about two thirds of the room, so fewer backgrounds must stay consistent (Katz ch. 7).
- Connect zones by following one person between them, or by a reverse where one zone's foreground person becomes the next zone's background person (Katz ch. 7).
- Show background extras early so a later cutaway to them reads as part of the scene (Katz ch. 4).
