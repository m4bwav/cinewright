# Dialogue triangle

## Vocabulary

- apex: side-on two-shot, both people on one plane. The triangle's tip.
- external: over-the-back reverse; both in frame, one facing camera.
- internal: between the two, one person only, not a POV.
- subjective: on the line itself; the other person's POV.
- parallel: profile single, camera axis parallel to the other side's.
- right-angle: two setups at 90 degrees, for an L-shaped pair.
- axis-advance: a closer shot on the lens line of an earlier card.

## Rules

- Write the setup name in the card notes. Every rule below depends on which pair of setups two cards form (Arijon ch. 4).
- Cut only pairs on the same side of the line. External pairs with external or internal. Internal and parallel cover one person each and never serve as the master.
- The apex two-shot is the weakest setup: both half-profile, equal space. Use it to open or close the exchange, as a pause, or before the cut pattern changes. Do not make it the only master.
- External reverse: the person facing camera takes about two thirds of the frame and is the speaker. The back-turned shoulder takes one third (ch. 4-5).
- Wide frames (2.39:1, or 16:9 at MCU and closer): put the featured face in the centre third in both reverses, shoulder in the outer third. The eye then does not swing across the screen at each cut.
- Sizes run in pairs, at most two pairs per exchange. Halfway, swap them (A medium and B close, then A close and B medium). The dominant person gets the closer size (ch. 5).
- A table or desk between the two: open the external reverses toward a right angle, not hugging the line (ch. 5).
- Heads set the line, not bodies. People lying down, back to back, or side by side in a car or on a bench still have a line between their heads. Cover a side-by-side pair with external, internal or frontal setups along it (ch. 4).
- The line need not be level. One lying and one standing: each reverse sits at its own person's head height. A balcony to the street gets a vertical triangle (ch. 5).

- A lone figure's line runs from the eyes to what they look at, even off screen.
- Looking straight ahead: never cut two side-on reverses; the gaze flips. Looking sideways: never cut a front and a back.
- A head turn of about 180 degrees is covered side-on; a 90 degree turn front and back (ch. 4).

## Verify

- Manual: two consecutive dialogue cards name setups on the same side; the speaker faces camera in an external.
