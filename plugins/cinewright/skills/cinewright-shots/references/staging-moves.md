# Staging moves

## Rules

- Space is territory. At each beat, note who advances (steps toward) and who yields (steps back, turns away, sits). The card's move shows it (Rabiger ch. 21, 24).
- A move has one of five reasons: utility, showing the space, showing an inner state, showing a relationship, or stressing a line (ch. 24).
- Avoidance: one keeps moving away as the other follows; each cross is a refusal. One cross per card (ch. 24).
- Regrouping is a reset. When people move to new places and facings, show the move; it starts a new axis and a new phase. Reactions from before and after cannot be swapped (ch. 24).
- Limit movement so each move matters. No milling: under half a scene's cards should carry a travel move (ch. 24).
- Stage a threat in one frame where you can, both parties together, rather than intercutting (ch. 24).
- A talk scene opens with movement that settles: both enter and stop, one arrives to the other, voices start over an empty set. It ends with the reverse (Arijon ch. 8).
- Walking toward or away on one side of the line: an object behind the walker in one card stays in the same frame half when it is in the foreground of the next. About two thirds of the path in one card, one third in the other (ch. 11).
- A journey between zones is three cards: close leaving (exit one side), full shot crossing the frame (enter the opposite side), close arriving. Four for long distances. More than five fragments tires. Dropping the middle and holding on the one who waits implies distance (ch. 12).
- A walker leaving one place and entering another makes the two read as neighbours. Use it when a model cannot draw both in one frame (ch. 12).
- A departure can end on the one who stays: cut on the exit to the stayer's close-up on the same lens line (ch. 16).
- Crossing the room to a new zone flips sides. Move the camera across the line at the new zone instead. If someone must cross the line in shot, make it the person nearest camera (Katz ch. 4).
- Geographical paragraphs: each dramatic block gets its own area, height or facing. Save one unseen part of the place for the climax ("change the stage"). Tag each scene's distance curve (`together->apart`, `apart->together`, accordion) (Proferes ch. 4).
- Facing marks a beat: sideways backs off, square-on pursues, turning away concedes (Proferes ch. 4).
- Character introductions: build a principal's entrance: back to camera or voice first, then a turn into the lens as the camera closes; never a slide-in (Sherman ch. 5; Proferes ch. 5).
- A new threat enters in close-up (`intro: cu`). This overrides coverage's "unreadable antagonist at FS" for the entrance card only (Sherman ch. 5).

## Verify

- Manual: each move in a card has a reason; a regroup has a card that shows it.
