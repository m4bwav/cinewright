# One movement phrase per shot

## Rules

- One phrase per shot: a single movement idea from a still start to a still or steady end (reach and take; rise and turn; three steps and a stop). A second idea is a second card.
- Write the phrase in the card's `action`; write where the body starts in `start_state` and where it ends in `end_state`. The end state is what the next card starts from.
- Rhythmic or repeated moves (dance, rowing, hammering): give a count ("three strokes, then still"), never "keeps rowing". An open-ended move drifts or loops.
- Subject and camera move in agreement or one of them holds still. A subject moving left while the camera pans right fails more often than either alone.
- Big moves go in wide shots, small moves in close shots. A full-body jump in an MCU leaves the frame; a finger tap in a WS is invisible.
- Choreography across shots: split a sequence into phrases, one per card, and cut on the action (match on action): end card A mid-motion, start card B with the same motion from the new angle.
- Moves that cross the frame follow the scene's travel direction (screen-direction entry).

## Numbers

- A phrase lasts 1-4 s. Shot 3-8 s. Counts: up to 4 repeats per shot.

## Vocabulary

- phrase, count, start state, end state, match on action, cut on action, hold, transition.

## Pitfalls

- "Then" in an action means two phrases (`continuity diff` ONE-ACTION warns).
- Asking for a long fight or dance in one generation produces moves that melt into each other; one exchange per shot.

## Verify

- Each card has one verb phrase in `action`, a `start_state` and an `end_state`; `continuity diff` shows no ONE-ACTION warning.
