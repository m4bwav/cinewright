# Weight, contact and follow-through

## Rules

- Write a movement in four parts, in one sentence: the start state, the verb with a speed, the contact (what touches what), the settle. "She sets the heavy bucket down hard on the stone floor; water slops over the rim and settles."
- Weight shows in effort and in what reacts: a knee bends, a shoulder drops, a surface gives, something spills. Name one reaction the weight causes.
- Contact: name the body part and the surface ("palm flat on the table", "boot heel into mud"). Unnamed contact floats or slides.
- Follow-through: loose things keep moving after the body stops: hair, a coat hem, a rope, water. Name one.
- Anticipation: a small move the opposite way before a big one (a wind-up, a crouch before a jump). Write it when the action is fast.
- Speed words a model follows: slowly, steadily, quickly, suddenly, in one sharp motion. Avoid "dynamic" and "epic".
- A hit or fall is sold by the reaction, not the contact: the head snaps, the body folds, the onlooker flinches. Put the reaction in the same shot or the next.
- Hands doing fine work (striking a match, tying a knot) are hard subjects: keep them large in frame and the motion simple (entry hard-subjects).

## Numbers

- One movement takes 1-3 s of a 3-8 s shot; leave time before it (start state) and after it (settle) so the clip does not end mid-motion.

## Vocabulary

- anticipation, action, contact, follow-through, overlapping action, settle, weight shift, effort qualities (Laban: weight, time, space, flow).

## Pitfalls

- Weightless motion (gliding walks, objects that drop slowly) comes from verbs with no effort; give the verb a load and a surface.
- Ending the clip at the moment of contact cuts the settle off; the cut then feels early.
