# cinewright-movement references

Generated by `cine.py kb index`; do not hand-edit. Open one entry with `python scripts/cine.py kb show <slug> --section <name>` (sections: Rules, Numbers, Vocabulary, Pitfalls, Verify, Notes).

- `battle-scenes`: Plan a battle as a map, two sides with fixed screen direction, three to five phases, and many small fights; build scale from a 3D blockout, tiled groups or few large figures, never a prompted mass.
- `crowd-blockout`: Stage an army or crowd in 3D (polywright pw.py crowd), render depth, pose and mask videos, and let the video model paint the look over them; the model never invents the mass.
- `fights-and-stunts`: Break a fight into single exchanges, one per shot, keep fighters on their sides of the axis, sell hits with reactions, and cut around contact rather than render it.
- `flying-and-levitation`: Write floating people as held up by nothing (never "as though from wires"), one continuous flight move per shot from a storyboard or first and end frames, take-off and landing as separate cards.
- `hard-subjects`: Animals, crowds, vehicles drawn by animals, hands and liquids break in video models; show them side-on, few and large, and give the mass one line in the background.
- `one-phrase-per-shot`: Each shot holds one movement phrase with a clear start and end state; split anything longer, count beats for rhythmic moves, and keep subject and camera from fighting.
- `screen-direction`: The fixed phrases for travel, eyeline and frame position that every card and compiled prompt uses, so direction never drifts between shots.
- `weight-and-contact`: How to write a movement so a video model renders weight - anticipation, contact point, follow-through and settle - and why the reaction sells a hit.
- `wounds-and-healing`: Show a wound appear or heal by pinning a wounded first frame and a clean last frame with only the wound changed, or by reversing a wounding shot; untested end to end.
