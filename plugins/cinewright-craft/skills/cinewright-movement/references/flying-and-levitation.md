# Flying and levitation

## Rules

- Say what holds the figure up, as a fact: "floating free, held up by nothing but sorcery". Video models take similes literally; "as though on wires" draws wires.
- Name the flight mode per character on the card: flying (the body leads, legs held together and trailing behind, toes pointed, leaning into the flight like a diver) or air-walking (striding on an invisible floor, as some sorcerers do). A flyer that takes walking steps on the air is a fault (code `air-steps`); an air-walker who drifts without stepping is one too. Write the pose positively; never "not walking".
- One flight move per shot: hover, take-off, a glide past the skyline, or a landing. Take-off and landing are separate cards (entry fights-and-stunts).
- State the direction of travel in frame words and let hair, cape and clothes stream the opposite way; that is what reads as speed (unverified on video models).
- For a hero in flight, use a storyboard image as a reference with a stated role ("shot progression and composition only; do not show the sketch") plus a character sheet. First and end frames help when the flight is one continuous move.
- Keep the flyer large in frame. A small dark upright shape in the sky reads as a person; anything that must not be a person needs a shape described without person words.
- Floating groups: say every member floats, or some will stand on the ground in the foreground (library film, 2026-10-06).

## Numbers

- Budget 20-30 takes for a showcase flight shot (one maker, 29+ for a single orbit around a floating hero, 2026-10).

## Vocabulary

- hover, take-off, glide, bank, landing, held up by nothing, streaming cape.

## Pitfalls

- "Levitating" alone often gives a figure standing on an unseen surface or on the ground; give the gap ("a body length above the floor"). Unverified.
- An orbiting camera plus a moving flyer melts the figure; let one of them move (from the fights rule; unverified for flight).

## Verify

- No wires, ropes or rigs in any frame; the feet clear the ground in every frame of a hover.
- A flyer's legs never make walking steps; an air-walker's feet land on one invisible level stride after stride.
