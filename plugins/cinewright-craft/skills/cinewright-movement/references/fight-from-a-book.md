# Fight from a book

## Rules

- Read only the fight. In a long book, search for the fighters' names and weapon words, count matches per chapter, and read those sections (a reader tool such as readwright does this without loading the book). Keep the place of each beat (section and paragraph or line) for the exchange's `src`.
- Pull design facts in the same pass: each fighter's build, height against the other, costume that night, weapons and where they are carried, injuries from earlier scenes, how skilled the text says they are, the room and its light. Write contradictions down once and rule on each in the design notes.
- List every physical beat in order, then sort it: an exchange; a line or look (goes in a pause); an inner beat (thought, memory, feeling). Turn each inner beat into something seen or heard, or cut it: "he knew he was outmatched" becomes parries a beat late.
- Prose summaries ("for a few seconds they fought furiously") become one specific exchange that shows what the summary claims. Mark it added: no `src`.
- Keep the turns exact: the wound, the disarm, the cheat, the line that changes who is ahead. Invent only between them.
- Repeated beats merge (two identical feints become one). Dialogue that stalls the action moves to the nearest pause.
- The world's rule of combat becomes the visible style in every card (a slow blade through a shield, a school of fencing someone mocks).
- Decide what to show of violence the text describes in detail; cut on contact for the worst of it (entry fights-and-stunts).

## Numbers

- About 100 words of fight prose per exchange; a 2,000-word duel gave 21 exchanges.

## Pitfalls

- Planning from memory of the book instead of the text: weapons change hands and wounds change sides.
- Keeping every interior beat as a pause: the fight stops for reflection the audience cannot see.
