# On-screen text and interfaces

## Rules

- Sort all text in a shot into two classes before design. Story text must be read. Background text must look real and not be read. Viewers read any word they can, so a clear background word pulls the eye off the actor.
- Generate plates and screens blank or with abstract panels. Models garble letters and the garble changes every frame, so add both classes in post on a tracked layer. This keeps the set-check rule against readable text.
- Story text: few words, large, high contrast, held long enough to read twice. Keep it in the same font and layout as the background text so size alone marks it as important.
- Do not make a viewer read while a character speaks. Time story text to a gap in the dialogue, and dim or remove the background text during speech. Treat this as a timing habit: the book's claim that nobody can do both at once is stronger than subtitle research supports.
- To keep background text unread, use one or more of these: abbreviations and letter-number codes, bare numbers, a script the audience does not read, an invented script, real computer code, reversed text on a transparent screen with a face behind it, the frame's edges, dense repeated fields, icons, or color and sound with no text at all.
- A foreign script or real code distracts those who read it. Check what it says, or prefer letter-number codes.
- Design screens at three scales: bold blocks that show structure in a wide shot, medium labels at mid distance, fine detail for a close-up. Fine detail alone turns to grey mush when wide.
- Fix one interface style in `style.json` for the film and keep it on every screen. Internal consistency persuades more than technical accuracy. A repeated layout also teaches the viewer to look only at what changes.
- Use a convention the audience already knows when explaining would cost attention: scan lines say screen, a stencil says military. Do not spend story time justifying it.
- Build a future from present things pushed further, not from a blank page. Name the real object or company type the prop extends in its description.
- A world's brand fits its owner. One corporation means one font and one logo everywhere, with color pulled back so it melts into the set.
- Fake future title logos stack effects (slant, slices, bevels, metal, star field). Each one dates the film; use them only for pastiche.

## Numbers

- Line budget for a story screen: one idea, about three to six words at the largest size. This is a working default, not from the book; test it on the real cut.

## Pitfalls

- Sci-fi screens serve the shot, not the user. Transparent displays and unreadable fine print are poor product interfaces; do not carry them into a game UI or web page without checking.
- Do not fill a must-read moment with a joke in small print. Keep jokes where the eye has already finished with the story.
- Eurostile and OCR fonts read as an older idea of the future. They are a cliche used on purpose or a date stamp, so choose them for that reason.
