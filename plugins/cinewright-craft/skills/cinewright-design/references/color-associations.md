# Color associations

## Rules

- Every reading below is book rule, unverified, culture-bound: one teacher's classroom observation and Western film habit up to about 2003. Ask each as a question against the plan; none is a fact about audiences.
- For each `visual_structure.meanings` entry, ask: does the chosen variant start with this reading or against it? Against: change the variant, or plan the teaching scene that earns the new meaning (Block's rule in visual-structure still holds).
- Red: does bright red mean power or threat here, on either side? Warm orange-red desire, rose romance, burgundy rank, pink harmless, a faint red power lost?
- Yellow: is saturated yellow meant as alarm or obsession? Pastel innocence, near white elegance, golden light memory, hard yellow light threat?
- Blue: is pale or grey-blue meant passive or sad? Cold bright blue control, steel or indigo restraint, turquoise open and sociable?
- Orange: opaque orange friendly and plain, amber light warm, an orange sky at midday bad air, earth tones home?
- Green: leaf green life; lime, acid or mould green sick or artificial; green light, haze, liquid or skin poison?
- Purple: is it an omen of loss, death or change, or the unseen? Rarely desire.
- Three modifiers bend any reading: the carrier (object, plant, light, air, liquid, skin), dark company (warm hues beside heavy black turn menacing), and warm or cool lean inside the hue.

## Pitfalls

- Picking a colour from an idiom (blue sky, so blue is hope) gets the bodily reading instead (tired, sad). Name what the colour does in the frame.
- Mourning, wedding and danger colours differ by culture. For a non-Western setting or audience, drop the table and teach meanings on screen.

## Verify

- Each meanings entry has a yes or no against its default, and every no has a changed variant or a named teaching scene in `design.md`.
