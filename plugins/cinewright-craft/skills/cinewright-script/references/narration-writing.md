# Narration writing

## Rules

- Write narration after the shot list exists, against the pictures in order. Written first, it describes what the viewer already sees.
- Each block adds what the picture lacks: a name, a date, a cause, a number. Never say what is on screen; never say what the picture contradicts.
- A name lands on the shot where that person or place first shows, not a block earlier or later.
- One idea per block. Size each block to the gap before the next line of sync speech or the next moment that must play clean; at 2.5 words a second, a 6 s gap holds about 15 words.
- Write for the ear: short sentences, subject before verb, no two numbers in one sentence, no words a voice trips on, no "the former and the latter".
- Do the sums. Ages, spans and totals said aloud must agree with each other and with on-screen text.
- No hype words ("incredible", "shocking"). Let the picture carry the weight.
- Leave strong moments without narration; silence over a reaction tells more than a line.
- Hold one point of view and one tense through the film.
- Plant what a later block will need, then remind the viewer briefly when it pays off.

## Numbers

- 2.5 spoken words a second at most (same rate as entry dialogue-for-generated-voices).

## Pitfalls

- A block that runs past its gap and steps on sync speech: trim the words, never speed the voice.
- Narration that explains a joke or a shock the picture has already landed.

## Verify

- Read every block aloud over its shots once. Any stumble or overrun gets rewritten.
