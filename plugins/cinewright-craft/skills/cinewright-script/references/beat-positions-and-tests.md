# Beat positions and tests

Book rules, unverified on generated films. Below about 2 minutes the fractions are guesses; the tests still hold. All warn only.

## Rules

- Catalyst: could the hero walk back to normal life after it? If yes, enlarge it (Brody ch. 2).
- Choice (break into two): the hero is the subject of an active verb on screen, not lured or pushed (Snyder ch. 4).
- Midpoint: a false win or false loss, the opposite of where the next stretch goes (Brody ch. 2).
- Midpoint, 2-5 min with a B relationship: the hero commits for real and the A and B stories meet, so the midpoint card includes the B character; warn `MIDPOINT-NO-B` by eye (Snyder 2009 ch. 3).
- Suspense (Iglesias 2005 p. 95): a suspense beat answers yes to all three: care (the viewer had an earlier reason to care for the character), likely (the threat is shown near: a clock, a distance, a count), open (the character has won and lost before). Fails: `suspense-no-care`, `suspense-unlikely`, `suspense-foregone`. By the catalyst the viewer knows the hero's goal, or the film runs on curiosity alone.
- Setup and payoff (Iglesias 2005 pp. 105-106, 136): list in `brief.md` each object, skill or line that solves the climax with the card that plants it; one first shown at the climax is deus ex machina. Before a turn that wins or loses something, a `cost:` line says what it costs this character.
- All is lost (2 min and up): bigger than the catalyst, the hero worse off than at the start, partly by their own doing (Brody ch. 2).
- Act two world: opposite to act one on a named visible axis, the bookend's or another (Brody ch. 2).
- Named failures (Snyder ch. 4, 6): laying pipe (set-up past about 23% before the hook); danger off screen (the threat or its consequence never shown); two kinds of magic (more than one invented rule to accept, and for AI films more invented things the model must hold steady); passive hero (the action happens to them).
- Scene jobs (Iglesias ch. 6, H26): list what each scene establishes; a scene with one job merges into a neighbour. Give each scene a seconds estimate and sum them against the target length before writing; over by more than about 10% (our threshold) cuts a scene, not words.
- Finale, 3 min and up (Snyder 2009 ch. 3): (1) gather team and tools; (2) the plan runs and minor characters with a shown flaw pay it off; (3) the surprise, caused by what the hero still wrongly believes, not chance; (4) dig deep: every outside means gone, the hero acts on `need:`, and the brief names which; (5) the new plan works. Warn `FINALE-NO-NEED` by eye when the climax beat shares no key word with `need:`. At 60 s-3 min keep (2) breaks, (4) wins (Brody ch. 2); at 30-60 s it is one beat: the solve uses the need.

## Numbers

Fraction of runtime. Default: Snyder's page positions over a 110-page script (ch. 4). Alternative: Brody's rounder novel percentages (ch. 2).

| Beat | Snyder | Brody | 60 s | 3 min |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| catalyst | 11% | 10% | 7 s | 20 s |
| break into two | 23% | 20% | 14 s | 41 s |
| midpoint | 50% | 50% | skip | 90 s |
| all is lost | 68% | 75% | skip | 122 s |
| break into three | 77% | 80% | skip | 139 s |

- Short subset (Snyder ch. 4): 30-60 s keeps opening image, catalyst, choice, one false high or low, final image. 2-5 min adds a midpoint, its mirrored all-is-lost and the finale. A 45 s film may open on the catalyst.

## Pitfalls

- Fractions used as gates. They are guides from features and novels.

## Verify

- By eye: each test above answered in `brief.md`; scene seconds summed within about 10% of the length.
