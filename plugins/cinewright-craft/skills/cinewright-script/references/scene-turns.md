# Scene turns

## Rules

- Every scene turns a value (McKee): name the value (trust, safety, hope, love, life) and its charge at the start and at the end, plus or minus. A scene that ends with the same charge it started with is cut or merged.
- Write three lines per scene in the script: the character's goal in the scene, what opposes it, and the turn (the moment the charge flips).
- The turn happens in one shot. Mark that card in its `beat` ("turn: she trusts him") and give it the insert or the close-up; the shots before it build to it, the shots after show the consequence.
- Turn on an action or a line the camera can see: a hand opens, a door shuts, a word is said. Never on a thought.
- Consecutive scenes alternate charge where possible (plus to minus, then minus to plus); two scenes turning the same way flatten the film.
- In a film of one scene, the scene turn is the story's turn.

## Numbers

- One turn per scene. A 10-60 s film has one scene turn; a 2-5 minute film has two or three.

## Vocabulary

- value, charge (plus or minus), goal, opposition, turning point, gap (between what the character expects and what happens), reversal, consequence.

## Pitfalls

- A turn spread over several shots reads as no turn in generated video, because each clip starts fresh. Put it in one shot.
- A turn by dialogue alone needs a reaction shot after it, or the audience does not see it land.

## Verify

- Each scene in `script.md` names its value with start and end charge, and the turn card is marked in its `beat`.
