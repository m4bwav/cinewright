# Revision and feedback

Book rules, unverified (the book collects what working writers say they do; nothing is tested). Sized for 45 s to 5 min films.

## Rules

- Fixed passes after the draft, in order (ch. 11, H53; ch. 14, H68): scene by scene (each turns and earns its place), the whole film (order, pacing, seconds against length), line by line (specific nouns and verbs, every line said aloud). One of each is enough for a short; feature habits of ten or more passes do not scale down.
- Fix the problems you already know before anyone reads the draft; a reader's time goes to what you cannot see (ch. 11, H56).
- A fresh reader per draft: someone who saw the last draft reads what they remember (ch. 11, H56). For an agent, a new subagent with only the brief and the script.
- Ask readers targeted questions: what does the lead want, where did you side with them, what did you feel at the turn. Compare with the feeling you meant per scene; a miss means it is in your head, not on the page (ch. 11, H56-57).
- Count notes across readers (ch. 11, H57): one reader, log it and decide; two, a warning; three or more, fix it.
- Keep the problem, not the reader's fix: log what the note says is wrong, then design the fix yourself. A note that would damage the film gets an answer with what it gains and loses, and an alternative (ch. 11, H57).
- Owner notes (from the person the film is for) are always fixed or answered; the count rule weighs other readers (house rule).

## Pitfalls

- Subagents on the same model with the same brief are not independent readers: their agreement is weaker evidence. Vary the brief, the questions or the model, or count a shared note once.
- Feeling good while writing proves nothing; the reader's answers decide (ch. 13, H66).

## Verify

- Before hand-off, a note in `brief.md` or the log names the three passes done, and lists each reader note with its count and what was done.
