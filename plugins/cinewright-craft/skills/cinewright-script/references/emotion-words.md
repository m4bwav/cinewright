# Emotion words

The verb stays the beat (entry performance-words). This entry is only for the face clause a close shot adds after verb and task.

## Rules

- Six primaries: anger, disgust, fear, joy, sadness, surprise (McCloud pp. 82-83). A card's face is one primary at a rung 1-4, or a blend of two.
- Write the rung word, never the primary: "indignation", not "angry". To push a take, step up exactly one rung (McCloud p. 84).
- Give the muscles, not the word: models follow a muscle clause better than an emotion name (McCloud p. 93; recipes below).
- Relate the whole head: a frown or anger compresses every part inward, a take or surprise opens brows, eyes and mouth apart (Thomas and Johnston p. 470).
- Physical states (pain, drowsiness, nausea, exertion, pleasure, relaxation, intoxication, glare or noise overload) are not emotions and combine with one: pain plus fear (McCloud p. 87).
- Emotional faces are symmetric. Asymmetry needs an outside cause on the card: a light, a sound, a blow (McCloud p. 86).
- Dialogue: name one attitude per shot before any lip-sync work, and reject a take with the wrong attitude before judging its sync (Thomas and Johnston p. 470).
- A dialogue shot over about 4 s plans one attitude change, placed on a word, with the eyes changing first (Thomas and Johnston p. 467).

## Numbers

| Primary | Rungs 1-4 | Muscle clause |
|---|---|---|
| anger | sternness, indignation, anger, rage | brows pulled down and together, upper lids raised in a glare, square open mouth |
| disgust | disdain, aversion, disgust, revulsion | brows down, eyes squeezed narrow, nose wrinkled up, lips pouted |
| fear | concern, anxiety, fear, terror | brows raised and drawn together, lids wide, lips stretched sideways and open |
| joy | satisfaction, amusement, joy, laughter | mouth corners pulled up and out, cheeks raised, eyes squinted into arcs |
| sadness | dejection, melancholy, sadness, grief | inner brows raised and knotted over squinted eyes, mouth corners pulled down |
| surprise | alertness, wonder, surprise, shock | brows straight up, lids wide, jaw dropped, rest of face slack |

Blends (McCloud p. 85): anger+disgust outrage; anger+fear cornered animal; anger+joy cruelty; anger+sadness betrayal; disgust+fear horror; disgust+sadness pained empathy; fear+joy desperation; fear+sadness devastation; fear+surprise spooked; joy+sadness faint hope; joy+surprise amazement; sadness+surprise disappointment. Mild joy plus mild sadness reads as pity (p. 86).

## Pitfalls

- A joy take with no squint or raised cheeks is a fake smile (`smile-without-eyes`).
- A big emotion where only the mouth moves reads dead (`face-only-mouth`).
- Good sync with the wrong attitude still fails; sync is judged second.

## Verify

- Each close-shot face clause holds a rung word or a muscle clause, not a bare primary.
- The qc look pass asks the face questions (entry look-pass).
