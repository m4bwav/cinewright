# Music spotting

## Rules

- Spot after picture lock (cinewright-edit). For each cue write in `sound/plan.md`: cue number, in and out timecode, what it does (tension, release, theme, time passing), diegetic or not, source and license.
- Start and stop cues on story beats (a look, a decision, a door) or hidden under a louder sound; a cue that starts on a picture cut feels mechanical.
- Under dialogue: keep the cue sparse in the dialogue's range (about 300 Hz-3 kHz), duck it 6-10 dB while lines play, or leave the line unscored.
- Less is more: an unscored scene makes the scored one count. Silence before a big cue gives it weight.
- Temp music is a guide for mood and tempo, never the final cue unless licensed.
- Generated music (cinewright pairs with a local music generator when one is installed): generate to the cue's exact length and tempo, then edit the ending on a bar.
- Rights: record the license of every cue, generated or library, before release.

## Numbers

- Ducking under dialogue: 6-10 dB. A cue's tail: fade over at least one bar or end on a hit.

## Vocabulary

- cue, spotting session, temp track, source music (diegetic), underscore, sting, stinger, button (the final hit), hit point.

## Pitfalls

- A wall-to-wall score: no contrast, dialogue buried.
- Mickey-mousing (music copying every action) outside comedy or animation.

## Verify

- Every cue in the cut has a row in `sound/plan.md` with timing, purpose and license.
