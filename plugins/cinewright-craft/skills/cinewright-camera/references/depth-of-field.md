# Depth of field

## Rules

- Depth of field grows with distance to the subject and a smaller aperture, and shrinks with a longer lens and a larger sensor. Close-ups on long lenses are shallow; wides on short lenses are deep.
- Default: shallow for singles and close-ups (the subject sharp, the background soft), deep for wides and anything where geography matters (where people stand must read).
- Say it in words in the card's `subject` or `notes`-driven action: "shallow depth of field, background soft" or "deep focus, foreground and background sharp". Models follow these phrases; they do not compute an f-stop. (Practice observation, unverified across models.)
- Keep depth the same for every card of one scene unless a focus change is the point.
- A rack focus (focus pull) is one move: give it a card of its own, from one named subject to another, with the camera static.
- Deep-focus looks (1940s, Citizen Kane style) need a wide lens and light everywhere; ask for "deep focus" and "sharp to the back wall".

## Numbers

- Shallow: 50mm and up at a close-up, wide aperture (f/1.4-f/2.8). Deep: 24-35mm at a wide, f/8 and smaller. For the cinematographer's notes only; prompts carry the words.

## Vocabulary

- shallow focus, deep focus, rack focus, focus pull, bokeh, background soft, split diopter.

## Pitfalls

- "Bokeh" alone often adds glowing balls of light to the background; use "background soft".
- Shallow focus on a moving subject can make the model blur the subject too; keep the subject's distance steady or use deeper focus.
- A focus pull in the same card as a dolly or a zoom is two moves.

## Verify

- Every card in a scene names the same depth, or its notes say why it changes.
