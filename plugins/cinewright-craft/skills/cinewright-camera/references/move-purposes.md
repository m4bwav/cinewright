# Move purposes

## Rules

- Every moving card names one job (Katz ch. 3):
  - emphasise: the camera approaches, the subject approaches, or both;
  - pick one: pan between people, approach two then isolate one, one steps ahead into close-up, one turns to camera;
  - transfer: follow A past B and stay on B, or pull back from a close-up to reveal a second person;
  - connect: follow from one space to the next, or track beside a walker.
- Prefer the subject approaching a still camera over the camera approaching. It is the cheaper and more stable route on a video model (ch. 5).
- Countering: the camera arcs against a mover's direction, motivated by the move, to relocate the view or cross the line. A quarter turn takes about 6 to 8 s (ch. 3, 5).
- Retracking: the camera may go back over ground it covered (in for some lines, out to a new area). For AI split it into two cards that cut on a rest (ch. 3).
- Far to near follows rising tension. Moving away and back in follows a rise and fall better than a lateral move (ch. 5).
- The pan is cheap and under-used. Use it for distance and remoteness (ch. 6).
- Hide a small zoom or reframe inside a lateral move, a pan, or a person crossing close to the lens (ch. 4, 6).
- A compound move is segments. One card per segment (ch. 4).

## Pitfalls

- A moving card whose beat names no job is a move for its own sake; hold the camera instead.

## Verify

- Manual: each card with a camera move names its job.
