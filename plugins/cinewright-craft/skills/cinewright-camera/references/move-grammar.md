# Move grammar

## Rules

- Move only when the scene's energy moves; a move with no reason at its start and its end is decoration. Never add `handheld` for energy alone (after Prieto in *FilmCraft*).
- 2D moves (pan, tilt, zoom) keep the space flat and calm. 3D moves (dolly, track, crane, arc) slide the near and far planes apart and add depth; use the move type as an intensity lever in the visual plan (after Block).
- Dolly against zoom: a dolly changes the perspective, so the background shifts against the subject; a zoom only enlarges the picture and reads as a look, not a step. A zoom suits a watcher's attention or a found-footage style; a dolly suits a character's change.
- `push-in` is a slow dolly toward a realization. Stop it on a composed frame and hold (`camera.settle`).
- `arc-left` and `arc-right` circle the subject at one distance: the background slides fast behind a slow subject. A slow arc puts a person on show; around two people it binds them.
- `dolly-zoom` stretches or squeezes the background behind a subject of fixed size. Once per film at most, for vertigo or a shock of recognition; a model may render it as a plain zoom (unverified), so check the take.
- Punch-in at the turn: on the beat the script marks as the scene's turn, cut from a wide straight to a much closer, longer-lens shot on the same axis. It is the one straight-in cut that is meant (the diff's STRAIGHT-IN warning can stay, with the reason in `notes`).
- Tracking alongside a walker at the same speed holds them; slower lets them pull ahead, faster leaves them behind (camera-moves vocab).
- A reveal moves past a foreground object or around a corner to show something new; the card's `beat` names what is revealed.
- Dominance picks the move in a confrontation. A steady push at the one retreating makes the audience the aggressor; a slow pull back from an advancing speaker makes the listener defensive; a held tight long-lens frame the actor lunges into is the most violent and the most reliable to generate (MS1 11.2-11.4). A push that breaks into a whip pan must ride a swung gun or fist at its exact speed (MS1 11.5). Book rules, unverified.

## Pitfalls

- A countermove (camera one way, subject the other) doubles the speed on screen; slow both.
- Video models speed up any move left unqualified: keep "slow" in the words.

## Verify

- Every moving card's `beat` says why it moves; `continuity diff` shows no SPACE warning for a 3D move in a flat scene.
