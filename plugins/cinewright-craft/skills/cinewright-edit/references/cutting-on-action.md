# Cutting on action

## Rules

- Cut while something moves (a hand reaching, a head turning, a door opening) and pick the motion up in the next shot. Motion pulls the eye across the cut and hides small mismatches of position or light.
- Cut about a third of the way into the movement: the start announces it, the rest completes in the new shot.
- Repeat 2-4 frames of the motion across the cut (an overlap), not zero; a perfectly continuous cut often looks like it skipped.
- The card pair must plan it with an overlap: card A completes the movement, and card B repeats its last 0.5-1 s from the new angle ("she sits down", again). The edit picks the cut point inside the overlap. Planning A to stop and B to start mid-motion relies on two takes agreeing on speed, which they rarely do (after Mascelli, *The Five C's*). cinewright-movement, entry one-phrase-per-shot, writes the phrase; this entry cuts it.
- Generated motion speeds differ between takes. If the incoming take is slower or faster, cut later in the slow one so the motion speed matches at the cut.
- Never cut on a still frame between two static shots of the same subject at similar size: that is a jump cut.
- A cut on a look or a reaction needs no motion match, so it is often safer between generations (entry cut-points).
- Hits generated in two halves: card A shows the strike low and close on the aggressor, card B the recoil on a long lens from low. Let A run one or two strikes, then cut to B on the next impact; the cut and the sound make the contact the model cannot (MS1 1.5). Book rule, unverified.

## Numbers

- Cut point: about 1/3 into the movement. Overlap at the cut: 2-4 frames at 24 fps. Overlap planned between the cards: 0.5-1 s of repeated action.

## Pitfalls

- The hand that reaches with the right hand in A and the left in B: motion hides position errors, not a changed hand (cinewright-continuity).
- Cutting at the end of a move after it has settled loses the cover the motion gave.
