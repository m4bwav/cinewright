# Draft cut review

## Rules

- Map the cut before judging it. One table: each section's start and end, its beat, its loudest second (ffmpeg `ebur128`, short-term), and its cut times (scene detection, entry cutting-around-bad-frames). Mark the second the main line of action (the train) starts.
- Read the map for shape: the loudest and busiest stretch should sit at the climax, and the cut rate should change across the film. A flat loudness line or an even cut rhythm means the film shows no build.
- Then look for the draft-cut failures:
  1. Too many beginnings: several openings in a row before the train moves. Keep one.
  2. False endings: a moment that feels final before the real end. Cut it or move it.
  3. Story switch: the film starts as one story and ends as another. Pick one; the brief's logline decides.
  4. Peach pits: a hard, interesting piece that does not belong. Cut it however good it is.
  5. No room: no beat without words or action for a reaction to land.
  6. Accusing juxtaposition: a cut that implies a person caused or meant something the record does not show (cinewright-script entry nonfiction-honesty).
- After a gap, say again in one sentence what the film is about now. Cut what does not serve that sentence.

## Numbers

- First trial: on a 148 s cut the map showed the transit section as loud as the crash (both about -13 LUFS short-term), a cut every 4 to 6 s from start to end, and the train starting at 44 s (30 percent in).

## Pitfalls

- Fixing a structure fault by trimming every shot: drop or move the section instead (entry pacing).
