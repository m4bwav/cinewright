# Reaction timing

## Rules

- Choose each exchange's reply as `clipped`, `natural` or `weighted`, and record the gap in frames in the cut table (Rabiger ch. 33).
- Clipped (impatient, hostile): trim the pause, or cut in before the line ends for an interruption.
- Weighted (the line landed): keep the pauses from both shots, or cut to the listener's reaction before the reply.
- In a heated exchange, cut to the person being acted on, mid-line, to see how they take it; cut back as they act in return. Speaker to speaker on every line is the anti-pattern (Rabiger ch. 16, ch. 33).
- A split edit favours the person whose picture comes in early or returns during the other's line. In a card set with a `pov`, lean the splits toward that person (Rabiger ch. 33).
- In an exchange of four lines or more, at least one cut is a J or L cut; straight cuts at every speaker change look planned.

## Numbers

- Starting guesses, not the book's: weighted gap 0.5 s or more; clipped 0.3 s or less; an interruption overlaps 4-8 frames.

## Pitfalls

- Generated dialogue runs lines tight. Ask for the pause in the card ("she does not answer at once") so there is a pause to keep or trim.
- A reply cut to the speaker who just finished, with no reaction, hides whether the line landed.

## Verify

- The cut table gives each reply a gap in frames, and weighted replies are 0.5 s or longer.
